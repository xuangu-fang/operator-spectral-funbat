\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

The method has exactly two components: \textbf{M1} maps operator knowledge at any level to a
bank of valid one-dimensional GP kernels; \textbf{M2} mixes that bank under a guaranteed
support floor.  Everything else --- the CP likelihood, the finite-Fourier variational
inference, the collapsed parameterisation, the routing granularity --- is model machinery or
an experimental control, and is deferred to \Cref{sec:inference} and the appendices.

\subsection{M1: from operator knowledge to a valid kernel bank}

\paragraph{M1-a: operator to joint spectrum.}
For a periodic constant-coefficient operator, $\widehat{u}(\wvec) = \widehat{w}(\wvec)/\Lhat(\wvec)$, so
\begin{equation}
  \Sop(\wvec;\theta) = \bigl|\Lhat_\theta(\wvec)\bigr|^{-2}\, \Sw(\wvec).
  \label{eq:joint-spectrum}
\end{equation}
The headline family is \emph{anisotropic reaction--diffusion}, with
$|\Lhat_\theta|^2 = (\kappa + D_x\omega_x^2 + D_y\omega_y^2 + D_t\omega_t^2)^2$ and unknown
$\theta = (\kappa, D_x, D_y, D_t)$ --- physically, ``we know it is diffusion, but the medium
was never characterised''. \todo{table with advection and wave as stress cases}

\paragraph{M1-b: nonnegative low-rank separation.}
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\lambda_q \ge 0,\; s_{dq} \ge 0}
  \Bigl\| \Sop - \sum_{q} \lambda_q\, s_{1q} \circ \cdots \circ s_{Dq} \Bigr\|_F^2 .
  \label{eq:nncp}
\end{equation}
The relative separation error $e_Q = \|\Sop - \widehat{S}_Q\|_F / \|\Sop\|_F$ is reported as
an \emph{a priori applicability diagnostic}, separately on the positive octant and on the
full signed grid.

\paragraph{M1-c: nonnegative spectra give valid kernels.}
For any nonnegative one-sided spectrum $s(k)$ on a periodic grid, define
\begin{equation}
  \phi_s(x) = \Bigl[\, \sqrt{s(0)},\;
  \bigl\{\sqrt{2s(k)}\cos(2\pi k x)\bigr\}_{k=1}^{K},\;
  \bigl\{\sqrt{2s(k)}\sin(2\pi k x)\bigr\}_{k=1}^{K} \,\Bigr].
  \label{eq:features}
\end{equation}
Then $k_s(x,x') = \phi_s(x)^\top \phi_s(x') = s(0) + 2\sum_{k\ge1} s(k)\cos(2\pi k (x-x'))$
is PSD by construction, and so is any nonnegative convex combination
$s_{dr} = \sum_q \route_{drq} s_{dq}$.  Hence nonnegative CP $\to$ parameter pooling $\to$
routing softmax $\to$ final kernel preserves PSD at every step.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Scope, kept in the main text.} $s_{dq}$ is a one-sided magnitude spectrum and real
Fourier features pair $+k$ with $-k$, so the represented covariance components are
\emph{axis-wise even and separable}.  The anisotropic-diffusion symbol is a sum of per-axis
squares and matches this exactly --- that is the \emph{mathematical} reason it is the
headline family, not merely that its numbers are better.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{M1-d: building the bank per knowledge level.}
K2 separates a single $\Sop(\theta^\star)$; K1/K0 draw $M$ parameter settings by Latin
hypercube from $\Theta_1$/$\Theta_0$, separate each into $Q_1$ atoms, and pool.  All of
$\Theta_1, \Theta_0, M, Q_1$ and any pruning rule are frozen before any test data is seen.
Pooling is viable only because the variational coefficient budget is independent of bank size
(\Cref{app:fairness}); otherwise K1 would win on parameter count alone.

\subsection{M2: mixture under a guaranteed support floor}
\begin{equation}
  \route_{drq} = \floorw_q + \Bigl(1 - \sum_j \floorw_j\Bigr)\softmax(\alpha_{dr})_q ,
  \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
with $\floorw_q = 0$ for operator atoms and a total of $25\%$ mass split evenly across the
generic atoms.  A convex combination cannot create support that no atom has, so without the
floor a misspecified operator prior is unrecoverable.  The floor trades a little
matched-prior accuracy for support safety, and its marginal value is predicted to
\emph{grow as the knowledge level drops}.
