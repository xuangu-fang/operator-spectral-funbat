\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The setting.}
A physical field is measured at a small random subset of grid entries --- a few
percent or less --- and the rest must be reconstructed.  Low-rank tensor models
exploit the multi-way correlation, and functional versions replace discrete
factor tables with continuous functions so unobserved coordinates can be
interpolated.  At a few percent observed, however, low-rank structure alone does
not determine the answer.  The model must still decide how smooth each factor is,
whether it oscillates, and whether different axes share a length scale, and there
is not enough data to decide those things reliably.

\paragraph{The information that is being left on the table.}
Standard functional tensor models answer those questions with a generic
Mat\'ern or RBF kernel, or by learning a kernel from the same scarce data.  Both
discard something that is almost always known even when the solution is not: the
\emph{form} of the governing equation.  A practitioner rarely knows the field,
often does not know the coefficients, and yet usually does know whether they are
looking at diffusion, transport, or a wave.  We show that the form alone is
enough to fix the kernel, and that fixing it beats learning it exactly where the
data is scarcest.

\paragraph{A prior, not a model.}
Our contribution is deliberately narrow.  The construction produces per-mode GP
spectra that drop into an existing functional tensor decomposition in place of
its generic kernels; model, capacity, optimiser and step budget are unchanged, so
every comparison in this paper varies only the origin of the spectra.  This also
means the result is not in competition with better tensor models --- it composes
with them.

\paragraph{Two details that turned out to be load-bearing.}
The eigenbasis of a stationary operator is fixed by its boundary condition, not
by its symbol alone.  Periodic domains give complex exponentials; no-flux
boundaries give cosines.  Real initial-value data is not periodic along time, and
imposing $f(0) = f(1)$ there is a large unphysical constraint
\todo{cite the measurement}.  Separately, the host model must be a Tucker rather
than a CP decomposition: real two-dimensional fields are fields of blobs, not
outer products, and are not low CP-rank at any rank that sparse observations
could identify, even though their multilinear rank is small.

\paragraph{When this cannot work, stated first.}
The method needs the operator to suppress a large part of the spectrum.  That is
the same condition that makes the field low multilinear rank, so the requirement
is not an extra assumption but the one that was already there.  Broad-spectrum
turbulence fails both halves at once, and we report it as a measured failure
rather than omitting it.  We screen candidate datasets against this criterion
before committing to them, and report the screen including what it rejected.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A construction taking the form of a PDE --- not its coefficients, not its
        solution --- to a positive semi-definite kernel for each mode of a
        functional tensor decomposition, with no kernel parameters left to learn.
  \item Evidence that fixing the kernel this way beats learning it from a generic
        dictionary precisely in the sparse regime, with the advantage shrinking as
        observations grow, which is the behaviour the claim predicts.
  \item An explicit applicability criterion, measurable before training, together
        with the datasets and operator families it rejects.
\end{enumerate}
