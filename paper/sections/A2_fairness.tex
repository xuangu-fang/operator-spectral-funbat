\section{Fairness control: collapsed vs.\ expanded mixtures}
\label{app:fairness}

The earlier parameterisation gave every atom its own coefficient vector,
$f_{dr}(x) = \sum_q \sqrt{\route_{drq}}\,\phi_{dq}(x)^\top a_{drq}$, which is correct as a
prior covariance but gives an $8$-atom bank twice the variational coefficients of a $4$-atom
bank --- so method comparisons absorb a parameter-budget difference.

The canonical collapsed form first mixes the spectra and then uses a single coefficient
vector, \Cref{eq:collapsed}.  Since all atoms share the same discrete Fourier support the two
prior covariances are identical; collapsing merely removes duplicated features.  The
variational coefficient count is $2DR(1+2K)$, \emph{independent of bank size}.

\begin{quote}
This is what makes the knowledge-ladder experiment possible at all: a K1 bank is $M$ times
larger than a K2 bank, so without collapsing, the level comparison would be entirely
confounded by parameter budget.
\end{quote}
