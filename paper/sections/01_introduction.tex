\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The setting.}
Consider monitoring a gas leak in a room, a combustion chamber, or a length of
pipe.  In each case the sensors go where they can be mounted --- on the walls, on
one accessible face, on a single instrumented patch --- and not where the
quantity of interest is.  The measurements therefore occupy a small, contiguous,
badly placed part of the domain, and the reconstruction is wanted everywhere
else.  This is not the missingness pattern the tensor completion literature
assumes.  Uniformly random masks leave every unobserved entry with observed
neighbours; a wall-mounted array leaves most of the room with none.

\paragraph{Why this changes what a prior has to do.}
Under random masks, reconstruction is interpolation, and a smoothness prior is
already an adequate description of it: nearby points are similar, and there is
always a nearby point.  Under a confined layout, reconstruction is extrapolation,
and smoothness degenerates.  A stationary kernel says only that correlation
decays with distance, so far from the sensors it says the field is near its mean
--- which is a statement about ignorance, not about the field.  Our own control
experiment shows both halves of this: with sensors anywhere, three quite
different methods tie to three decimal places; confine them to one wall and they
spread out by a wide margin.

\paragraph{The information that is being left on the table.}
What a smoothness prior lacks in the unobserved region is any statement of what
the values there should be.  For a dissipative operator the governing equation
supplies exactly that.  Its symbol fixes how strongly each spatial and temporal
frequency is suppressed, which is a direct statement about how the field decays
along each axis --- and decay along an axis is what one-directional extrapolation
needs.  A practitioner rarely knows the field, often does not know the
coefficients, and yet usually does know whether they are looking at diffusion,
transport, or a wave.  We show that the form alone is enough, and that it earns
its keep exactly where the geometry of the sensors makes the problem hard.

\paragraph{A prior, not a model.}
Our contribution is deliberately narrow.  The construction produces per-mode GP
spectra that drop into an existing functional tensor decomposition in place of
its generic kernels; model, capacity, optimiser and step budget are unchanged, so
every comparison in this paper varies only the origin of the spectra.  This also
means the result is not in competition with better tensor models --- it composes
with them.

\paragraph{On not tuning the baselines down.}
The comparison is only interesting if the alternatives are given every
advantage.  We hand the Mat\'ern arm its length scale by minimising the
\emph{held-out} error, per seed and per layout, over a grid we check brackets its
own optimum; that is an oracle no deployable method has.  Our arm is given
nominal coefficients that are wrong by $50\%$ and no tuning data at all.

\paragraph{Two details that turned out to be load-bearing.}
The eigenbasis of a stationary operator is fixed by its boundary condition, not
by its symbol alone.  Periodic domains give complex exponentials; no-flux walls
give cosines --- and no-flux is what a room actually has.  Separately, the host
model must be a Tucker rather than a CP decomposition: real two-dimensional
fields are fields of blobs, not outer products, and are not low CP-rank at any
rank that sparse observations could identify, even though their multilinear rank
is small.

\paragraph{When this cannot work, stated first.}
The method needs the operator to suppress a large part of the spectrum, which is
the same condition that makes the field low multilinear rank, so the requirement
is not an extra assumption but the one that was already there.  It additionally
needs the sensors to be able to see the region of interest at all: a field driven
by independent noise everywhere is not determined by its boundary, and a single
narrow plume reaches the wall attenuated below the noise floor.  Both were
rejected by pre-declared screens before any method was run, and we report them.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A construction taking the form of a PDE --- not its coefficients, not its
        solution --- to a positive semi-definite kernel for each mode of a
        functional tensor decomposition, with no kernel parameters left to learn.
  \item Evidence that the value of that prior is controlled by \emph{sensor
        geometry} rather than by sample count alone: it is worth nothing when
        samples are scattered and most when they are confined to one face, which
        is the behaviour a per-axis spectral prior predicts.
  \item An explicit applicability criterion, measurable before training, together
        with the datasets and sensor geometries it rejects.
\end{enumerate}
