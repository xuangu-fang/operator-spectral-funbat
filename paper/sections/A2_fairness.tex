\section{Baseline fairness: auditing the grids, and what the audit found}
\label{app:fairness}

Every comparison in this paper rests on baselines that were given their best
chance.  Two mechanisms are involved, and one of them failed at first.

\subsection{Parameter budget}
The earlier parameterisation gave every atom its own coefficient vector,
$f_{dr}(x) = \sum_q \sqrt{\route_{drq}}\, \phi_{dq}(x)^\top a_{drq}$, which is a
correct prior covariance but gives an $8$-atom bank twice the variational
coefficients of a $4$-atom bank -- so a method comparison would absorb a
parameter-budget difference.  The collapsed form mixes the spectra first and then
uses a single coefficient vector, \Cref{eq:collapsed}.  Because all atoms share
the same discrete support the two prior covariances are identical and collapsing
merely removes duplicated features; the variational coefficient count is
$2DR(1 + 2K)$, independent of bank size.  Every table in this paper uses the
collapsed form for all arms.

\subsection{Hyper-parameter grids, and a retraction}
The Mat\'ern arm selects its length scale by minimising error, which is a
safeguard only if the grid contains the optimum.  Ours did not.  The original
grid was $(0.12, 0.32, 0.8)$, chosen when the experiments used random masks,
where it is more than adequate.  Under a one-wall layout the baseline's optimum
lies far outside it.

An earlier draft of this paper reported a $+17.2\%$ margin over the Mat\'ern arm
at one wall.  That margin was the distance between our arm and a baseline
searching too narrow a grid, and it does not survive the correction: against the
properly bracketed optimum the two arms are level.  We report this rather than
quietly rerunning, because the failure mode is worth naming.  Tuning a baseline
\emph{up} until a comparison looks good is an intervention a reader can see in
the method section.  Tuning it too \emph{little} produces a number that looks
like a measurement, and nothing in the experiment flags it.  The safeguard is
cheap: sweep wide enough that the chosen value is interior, and report that it
is.  All grids in this paper are now audited that way, and the main table's
grid $(0.12, 0.32, 0.8, 1.6, 2.4, 3.5)$ brackets its optimum on every layout.

\subsection{Two tiers, because the oracle is not available}
The audited oracle tier is reported throughout but marked $\star$: it selects
against the true held-out region, which no practitioner can do.  Alongside it we
report a deployable tier that selects on a random quarter of the sensor readings
over the same grid, with the same host, ranks, optimiser and step budget.  The
gap between the two tiers is the paper's main quantity, and it is a property of
the sensor geometry rather than of any method: it is $+0.0003$ under random masks
and $+0.2697$ at a single wall.
