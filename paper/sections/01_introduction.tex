\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Setting.} Multi-dimensional physical fields are often observed at only a handful
of sensors, times, or operating conditions.  Low-rank CP/Tucker exploits the multi-way
correlation, and functional tensor models replace discrete factor tables with continuous
functions so that unobserved coordinates can be interpolated.  At $1$--$5\%$ observation
rates, however, low-rank structure alone is not enough: the model must still decide how
smooth each continuous factor is, whether it oscillates, and whether different coordinate
directions should share a length scale. \todo{tighten}

\paragraph{The question we actually ask.} Standard functional Bayesian tensor models answer
those questions with a generic Mat\'ern or RBF kernel, discarding an information source that
is almost always available.  We do not ask whether the solution is known --- we ask
\emph{how much is known about the operator that governs the field}.  That admits a ladder
(\Cref{tab:ladder}), and the level that matters in practice is not the top one.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Operator-knowledge levels.  Prior physics-informed GP work effectively assumes
  K2; K1 is where most applications sit.}
  \label{tab:ladder}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{llp{0.42\linewidth}}
    \toprule
    Level & What is known & Example \\
    \midrule
    K2   & PDE form \emph{and} coefficients & calibrated rig; simulation with visible generator parameters \\
    K1   & PDE form only, coefficients unknown & known to be anisotropic diffusion, but the medium's diffusion tensor was never measured \\
    K0   & physics class only & ``roughly diffusion-dominated transport'' \\
    K$-1$ & nothing & falls back to a generic GP kernel \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent The claim K1 licenses is one sentence: \emph{we do not need you to know the
coefficients, only which equation it is.}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A single construction from any knowledge level to valid per-mode GP kernels.}
        A nonnegative low-rank projection turns the non-separable $D$-dimensional operator
        spectrum into per-mode kernels, preserving positive semi-definiteness at every step;
        K1 is realised by pooling atoms over a parameter family (\Cref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{A fixed generic support floor as insurance for descending the ladder.} A
        convex combination cannot create frequency support that no atom has, so a
        non-closable fraction of generic spectral mass is reserved, yielding a quantified
        robustness--specificity tradeoff.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What we do \emph{not} claim.} We do not claim atom-label identifiability, PDE
discovery, or coefficient recovery from $2\%$ observations.  Routing granularity and the
collapsed parameterisation are an ablation and a fairness control respectively, not
contributions. \todo{cite the top-1 recovery audit}
