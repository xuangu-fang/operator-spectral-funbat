Reconstructing a physical field from a very small fraction of its entries is
underdetermined, and low-rank structure alone does not resolve it: the model must
still decide how smooth each continuous factor is along each axis.  We observe
that one piece of information is almost always available even when the solution
is not --- the \emph{form} of the partial differential equation the field obeys
--- and that it is enough.  From the form alone, with coefficients set to
nominal values, the operator's symbol gives the solution's joint power spectrum;
projecting that spectrum nonnegatively onto per-axis factors yields a valid
positive semi-definite GP kernel for every mode of a functional tensor
decomposition, with no kernel parameters left to learn.

The construction is a prior, not a model: it replaces the generic kernels in an
existing functional tensor method, so model, capacity and optimisation budget
are held fixed and the only variable is where the spectra come from.  Two details
turn out to be load-bearing rather than incidental.  The eigenbasis of a
stationary operator is fixed by its boundary condition, so real initial-value
data --- which is not periodic in time --- needs the no-flux cosine basis rather
than a periodic one.  And the host must be a Tucker rather than a CP model,
because real two-dimensional fields are not separable across axes at any rank
sparse observations could identify.
