\section{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:problem}

We observe a physical field on a regular grid at a small random subset of
entries and must reconstruct the rest:
\begin{equation}
  y_i = u(x_{i1},\dots,x_{iD}) + \epsilon_i,
  \qquad \epsilon_i \sim \Norm(0,\sigma^2),
  \qquad i \in \Obs,
  \label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}
with $|\Obs| / N_{\mathrm{full}}$ between $1\%$ and $5\%$.  Besides those
entries, the only information used is the \emph{form} of the PDE the field
obeys -- not its coefficients, and not the solution.

\paragraph{When this can possibly work.}
Two conditions are the same condition, and we state them before any experiment.
The operator must suppress enough of the spectrum for its prior to carry
information, and the field must then be low enough in multilinear rank for the
host model to represent it.  Dissipative operators satisfy both; broad-spectrum
turbulence satisfies neither, and \Cref{sec:limitations} reports it as a
measured failure rather than omitting it.

We screen candidate datasets on this criterion \emph{before} committing to them,
using the error of a fully observed low-rank fit, and report the screen
including what it rejected.
